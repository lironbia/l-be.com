Accessibility

Accessibility statement

I attach great importance to equal service for every person. I have invested in making the site accessible so that everyone can use it comfortably, with dignity and independently, with any disability and with any assistive technology.

Accessibility level

The site has been adapted to the W3C's WCAG 2.2 guidelines and to Israeli Standard 5568, at level AA. Beyond that, it has also been adapted to most of the requirements of level AAA, the strictest level.

The site was tested with an automated testing tool and by manual testing, page by page.

What has been done on the site

Navigation and structure

  • Everything on the site can be reached with the keyboard alone, and the current position is marked with a clear outline.
  • At the top of every page there is a "Skip to main content" link.
  • The structure of the pages is marked up for screen readers: headings, navigation regions, lists and tables.
  • The menu and the footer are the same on all pages, and the current page is marked in the menu.
  • A link that opens in a new tab or in a pop-up window is marked as such.

Vision

  • Text contrast is at least 7:1.
  • The text can be enlarged up to 200% without losing content, and the site adapts itself to any screen width.
  • Every image that carries information has a description. The screenshots of client messages are transcribed in full, and can also be read as plain text.
  • Information is not conveyed by color alone.
  • The site respects the operating system's high-contrast mode.

Hearing

  • The site has no audio or video content, and no sounds.

Motor skills

  • Buttons and links are large enough to click comfortably, at least 44 pixels.
  • There are no actions that require dragging, complex gestures or fast clicking.
  • The site has no time limits.

Understanding and attention

  • There is no flashing, and all the animations can be stopped. The site also respects the operating system's "reduce motion" setting.
  • There is a shortened version of the home page ("Short version"), for those who want to read less.
  • Abbreviations and professional terms are explained in the glossary at the end of this page.

Forms

  • Every field has a label, and required fields are marked with the word "required".
  • The error messages are written in words, appear next to the field and are read out by the screen reader.

The accessibility menu

In the corner of the screen there is a round button that opens the accessibility menu. The settings are saved in the browser for your next visit, and can be reset at any moment.

  • Making the text larger and smaller
  • High contrast
  • Dark display (inverted colors)
  • Grayscale
  • Readable font
  • Increased text spacing
  • Highlighted links
  • Stopping animations
  • Large mouse cursor

Keyboard navigation

  • Tab moves to the next link, button or field. Shift + Tab goes back to the previous one.
  • Enter activates a link or a button. Space activates a button.
  • Esc closes the accessibility menu and the portfolio window.
  • The arrow keys scroll the page.

The site is built with standards-compliant code, so that it works in the common browsers and with screen readers.

What is not yet perfect

  • The portfolio is a PDF file in English, and it has not been made fully accessible. You can ask me for its content in any other form.
  • Booking a call is done through an external service, Calendly, whose accessibility is not under my control. Instead, you can leave your details in the contact form.
  • The logos of clients and partners are images. Each of them has a description.
  • The error page (404) has a small game. It is just for fun, and all the links on the page are available without it.

I keep improving the accessibility of the site all the time.

Glossary of abbreviations and terms

CRM (Customer Relationship Management)
A system for managing the relationship with customers and leads.
AI (Artificial Intelligence)
Artificial intelligence.
AI agent
AI-based software that carries out tasks by itself, for example answering inquiries.
Automation
Actions the system carries out by itself, without anyone having to remember to do them.
Integration
A connection between two systems, so that information moves between them by itself.
Specification
A document that describes what a system needs to do, for whom and how, before it is built.
API (Application Programming Interface)
An interface through which software systems talk to each other.
SaaS (Software as a Service)
Software that you use over the internet, without installing it.
ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)
A system for managing the resources of the business, such as inventory, purchasing and finance.
BI (Business Intelligence)
Reports and dashboards that show what is happening in the business.
BPM (Business Process Management)
Business process management.
POS (Point of Sale)
The register, the point of sale.
SLA (Service Level Agreement)
A commitment to handling times and to a level of service.
UI (User Interface)
The user interface: what you see on the screen.
LLM (Large Language Model)
A large language model, the technology behind AI tools that understand and write text.
Roadmap
A plan of the road ahead: the order of the stages in building a system, and what goes into each stage.
Sprint
A short, fixed stretch of work, usually a week or two, that ends with a deliverable.
PDF (Portable Document Format)
A type of document file.
